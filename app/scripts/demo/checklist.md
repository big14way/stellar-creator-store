# Selling digital products for USDC: a checklist

Twelve steps from a finished file to your first sale and your first cash-out.
Written for the Paperstall demo shop. Free to share and change (CC0).

## Before you list

**1. Make the download complete on its own.**
Put everything a buyer needs in one file: the work itself, a short README that
says what is inside, and the licence. A buyer who has to message you to make
sense of the download will ask for a refund.

**2. Name the file for the buyer, not for you.**
`lagos-nights-beat-pack.zip` beats `final_v7_REAL.zip`. The name is kept when
the buyer downloads it.

**3. Say what they can do with it.**
Personal use only? Use in client work? Resell? One line in the description and
the same line in the README saves arguments later. If you are not sure, start
narrow; you can loosen the licence later, but you can't take back what you gave.

**4. Keep it small enough to download on a phone.**
Many buyers are on mobile data. Compress images, export audio at the quality
people actually use, and split very large packs into volumes.

## Pricing

**5. Price in dollars, think in your currency.**
USDC is a dollar stablecoin, so a price of 5 USDC means the same thing to a
buyer in Accra, Lagos, Nairobi or London. Check what that is in your currency
today, and look again when the exchange rate moves a lot.

**6. Know what you keep.**
Before you list, add up every cut: the platform fee, any co-creator shares and
the affiliate share on referred sales. On Paperstall the listing form shows the
split of one sale before you publish, and the shares are fixed in the contract
at listing time.

**7. Start with one low-priced product.**
A small product that sells shows the next buyer that people pay for your work.
Your first sales also show you which descriptions and prices work.

## Working with others

**8. Agree on splits in writing first.**
If someone helped make the product, agree their percentage before you list and
add their wallet as a co-creator. Each sale then pays everyone at once, and
nobody has to trust anybody to send money on later.

**9. Give affiliates a reason to share.**
An affiliate share only applies when a sale comes through someone's link. A
10 to 20% share is common for digital products. Tell the people who already
talk about your work that the share exists.

## After the sale

**10. Keep your receipts.**
Every sale has a transaction you can look up on a block explorer. Note the
date, the amount and the buyer's address for your records and for tax.

**11. Cash out on a schedule, not on every sale.**
Each withdrawal to a bank can carry fees. Pick a day of the week or month,
compare what the cash-out services on offer charge, and move money in larger
amounts. Check the service's limits and verification rules before you need
the money.

**12. Answer questions in public.**
When a buyer asks something, add the answer to the description and the README.
The next buyer reads it before they ask, and before they decide.

---

Questions this list doesn't answer are worth writing down. They are usually
the next thing to put in your product description.
